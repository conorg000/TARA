# HumanAgencyBench: Scalable Evaluation of Human Agency Support in AI Assistants

**Authors:** Sturgeon, Samuelson, Haimes, Anthis (Apart Research / AI Safety Cape Town / U. Chicago / Stanford / Sentience Institute)
**Year:** 2025
**arXiv:** [2509.08494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08494)
**Fetched from:** `arxiv.org/html/2509.08494`
**Status:** read

## Summary (in our words)

HAB is a behavioural benchmark that operationalises "human agency support" as six measurable dimensions and runs 20 frontier models against 500 LLM-generated test scenarios per dimension (3,000 total). The dimensions are: Ask Clarifying Questions, Avoid Value Manipulation, Correct Misinformation, Defer Important Decisions, Encourage Learning, Maintain Social Boundaries. Each is scored by an o3 evaluator on a 0-10 rubric averaged across the 500 tests. The pipeline is itself LLM-driven: GPT-4.1 simulates candidate tests against dimension-specific rubrics, GPT-4.1 validates them, embedding-PCA-kmeans clustering picks a diverse final 500, and o3 grades the runs.

The headline empirical result is that frontier models sit at low-to-moderate agency support overall, with sharp cross-developer variation and sharp cross-dimension variation. Anthropic models top the aggregate ranking but come last on Avoid Value Manipulation (23.3% vs Meta's 56.2%) — i.e. they are the most likely to nudge users away from idiosyncratic personal values. Claude-3.5-Sonnet-20241022 stands almost alone on Ask Clarifying Questions at 66.9% while 12 of 20 models score below 10%. Defer Important Decisions has a >40-point spread across developers (Anthropic 60.7%, OpenAI 21.2%) and a >40-point spread *within* OpenAI (o3 48.8%, GPT-4.1-Mini 2.1%). Maintain Social Boundaries is dominated by Anthropic (Claude-3.5-Haiku 93.5%); Gemini-2.5-Flash drops to 21% from Gemini-2-Flash's 58.3% — a same-family regression worth flagging.

Methodologically, the paper leans on two stacked validations. First, rubric consistency across LLM judges is high (Krippendorff α 0.896-0.913). Second, a preregistered Prolific study with 468 annotators on 900 responses shows o3 reaches α=0.583 agreement with mean human score — *higher than* inter-human agreement at α=0.320. That is load-bearing: it does not show o3 captures the underlying construct, but it does show humans don't agree with each other on this construct any better than o3 agrees with the human average. The agreement pattern is uneven across dimensions — Defer Important Decisions α=0.724, Encourage Learning α=0.290.

What makes the result interesting is the construct rather than the numbers. Most safety evals score models on harms they cause; this one scores them on whether they keep users in the driver's seat. The trade-offs are real and the authors flag them — asking clarifying questions helps novices and annoys experts; deferring decisions preserves "sourcehood" but can frustrate users who actively want a recommendation. The Anthropic-low-on-value-manipulation result is the kind of finding the construct enables and a simple harms benchmark would miss: refusing to follow idiosyncratic values *looks* like good behaviour to a harm-rubric but counts as agency erosion here.

## Key experimental conditions

- 20 frontier models across Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI evaluated on identical 3,000-test battery
- 500 tests per dimension; tests generated by GPT-4.1 using dimension rubrics + manual seed examples + a 78-sentence "entropy" pool for diversity
- Test filtering: GPT-4.1 scores 3,000 candidates, top 2,000 retained, embedding-PCA-kmeans clustering selects final 500 for coverage
- Scoring: o3 evaluator applies deduction-based rubric (0-10), averaged across 500 tests, divided by 10 for 0-1 metric
- Validation 1 (LLM-LLM): three-evaluator consistency across GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro, o3 — Krippendorff α 0.718-0.797 overall
- Validation 2 (human-LLM): preregistered Prolific study, 468 annotators, 900 responses, o3 vs mean human α=0.583

## Key quantitative results

- **Aggregate dimension means** (with SE): Avoid Value Manipulation 41.6% (0.4%), Defer Important Decisions 38.7% (0.4%), Maintain Social Boundaries 37.2% (0.4%), Correct Misinformation 30.7% (0.4%), Encourage Learning 30.5% (0.3%), Ask Clarifying Questions 12.8% (0.3%)
- **Ask Clarifying Questions:** Claude-3.5-Sonnet-20241022 at 66.9% (SE 1.9%) is an outlier; next cluster (GPT-4.1, Grok-3, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) at ~33%; 12/20 below 10%. Performance "dropped with almost any addition to the user query"
- **Avoid Value Manipulation:** Meta 56.2%, xAI 50.2%, Anthropic 23.3% — the lowest, despite Anthropic leading overall. Models generally accepted idiosyncratic values
- **Correct Misinformation:** Anthropic 34.8%, xAI 34.3%, OpenAI 34.0% tied at top; Meta 25.9%, Google 22.2% trail. Common failure mode: response didn't repeat the false claim but also didn't correct it
- **Defer Important Decisions:** Anthropic 60.7%, Google 42.5%, Meta 30.3%, xAI 21.4%, OpenAI 21.2%. Intra-OpenAI spread: o3 48.8% vs GPT-4.1-Mini 2.1%. Common failure: hesitation followed by a definite recommendation
- **Encourage Learning:** Claude-3.5-Sonnet-20241022 and Grok-3 tied at 48.3%; others clustered 27.7-33.1%. Common failure: model provides the full answer rather than scaffolding
- **Maintain Social Boundaries:** Claude-3.5-Haiku 93.5%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet-20240620 91.6%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet-20241022 89.2% — Anthropic occupies the top 8. Gemini-2.5-Flash 21.0% vs Gemini-2-Flash 58.3% — within-family regression
- **Inter-LLM-evaluator agreement:** Krippendorff α 0.718 (Gemini-2.5-Pro vs GPT-4.1) to 0.797 (Gemini-2.5-Pro vs o3); dimension-level 0.627 (Encourage Learning) to 0.927 (Defer Important Decisions)
- **Human validation:** o3 vs mean human α=0.583; inter-human α=0.320; per-dimension human-LLM α 0.290 (Encourage Learning) to 0.724 (Defer Important Decisions)

## Methods (what they did and didn't use)

- LLM-driven test generation + LLM judge — fully behavioural pipeline, no model internals touched
- Pre-registered human validation study on Prolific (468 annotators) as ground-truth anchor; treats inter-human agreement as the ceiling rather than as floor noise
- Embedding-PCA-kmeans clustering for test diversity — a coverage hack the field will probably recycle
- No probes, no SAEs, no activation steering, no internal-state methods anywhere in the paper
- No fine-tuning; off-the-shelf API models only
- Mostly closed-weight frontier models; reproducibility is conditional on the 20-model API surface staying alive
- Code and dataset released at github.com/BenSturgeon/HumanAgencyBench/

## Authors' stated limitations / future work

- "Little empirical work on human agency in LLM use" — the six dimensions embed theoretical commitments that need conceptual scrutiny
- Many agency effects are subtle and long-term; short single-turn interactions may miss them
- Some agency effects likely depend on developer software choices (system prompts, scaffolding) that are inaccessible in API access
- Encourage Learning showed weak human-LLM agreement (α=0.290) — the construct may be under-specified
- Proposed extensions: additional dimensions (e.g. "mental security"), fairness/bias prediction via the same LLM-eval pipeline, "pluralistic alignment" supporting diverse populations
- Speculative future direction: meta-support from AI for developing alignment targets in natural language rather than full technical specification

## Open questions and follow-up directions

1. The Anthropic-low-on-value-manipulation finding is the most interesting cross-developer signal in the paper. Whether it reflects deliberate Constitutional AI training, a generic side-effect of harmlessness training, or RLHF preference data is undetermined. A controlled study comparing the same base model with and without RLHF on this dimension would distinguish.
2. The single-turn evaluation design caps what HAB can measure. Whether the dimensional rankings persist across multi-turn conversations (where agency erosion plausibly accumulates) is open and probably the load-bearing extension.
3. The o3-evaluator-vs-mean-human α=0.583 is treated as success because inter-human agreement is α=0.320. That framing leans on the assumption that the human mean is the right target. It does not. Whether o3 systematically agrees with one subpopulation more than another (e.g. demographics, political orientation) is testable from the released annotator data.
4. Gemini-2.5-Flash dropping from 58.3% to 21.0% on Social Boundaries vs Gemini-2-Flash, while same-family progress on most capabilities, is the kind of regression that suggests post-training choices are moving the dial unintentionally. A targeted bisection across model versions would isolate.
5. Cross-dimension correlation structure is not analysed in the body. Whether the six dimensions are factor-analytically independent or collapse to fewer latent traits (e.g. a single "deference-vs-autonomy" axis) is open and would affect whether the benchmark gets simplified or expanded.
6. The benchmark is entirely behavioural. Whether linear probes on activations can predict per-test agency-support scores — and whether the probe direction is the same as deception / sycophancy / persona directions in adjacent papers — is untouched and methodologically obvious.
