# OR-Bench: An Over-Refusal Benchmark for Large Language Models

**Authors:** Justin Cui, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ion Stoica, Cho-Jui Hsieh (UCLA / UC Berkeley)
**Year:** 2024
**arXiv:** [2405.20947](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20947)
**Fetched from:** `arxiv.org/html/2405.20947` (v5)
**Status:** read

## Summary (in our words)

OR-Bench is the first large-scale benchmark targeting **over-refusal** — the failure mode where a safety-tuned model rejects a perfectly benign prompt because something about its surface form pattern-matches to "unsafe." Cui et al. build a three-stage LLM pipeline (toxic seed generation → rewrite-to-look-suspicious → ensemble moderation) that ends up with 80,000 prompts which are safe on close reading but look harmful at a glance, plus a 1,000-prompt hard subset (kept only if at least 3 of the largest models in each family refuse it) and a 600-prompt verified-toxic comparison set. The whole thing is generated by Mixtral 8×7B (chosen because it has weaker safety RLHF and will produce the seeds), filtered by majority vote across GPT-4-turbo, Llama-3-70B, and Gemini-1.5-Pro.

The headline finding is the **safety / over-refusal trade-off**: Spearman rank correlation of 0.89 between a model's toxic-prompt rejection rate and its over-refusal rate. Claude-3-Opus rejects 99%+ of genuinely toxic prompts but also rejects 91% of OR-Bench-Hard prompts; GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 rejects only 62% of toxic prompts but over-refuses just 12.7%. Claude-3.5-Sonnet is one of the few models that visibly moves off the diagonal — over-refusal drops to 43.8% on the Hard set vs. Opus's 91%. The GPT-3.5 versioning story is striking: -0301 over-refuses at 57.4%, -0613 sits in the middle, -0125 drops to 12.7%, suggesting OpenAI explicitly tuned it down across releases.

The other useful piece is the ablation that **defense methods make the trade-off worse, not better**. In-Context Learning defenses give the largest jailbreak-defense gain but also the largest over-refusal increase; SmoothLLM moves both in the same direction at smaller magnitude. The "be helpful and safe" system-prompt instruction flips GPT-3.5 into rejecting ~35% more toxic prompts and ~55% more benign ones — the helpful/safe instruction pushed safety up *less* than it pushed over-refusal up. Temperature 0.0-1.0 doesn't matter, which is what you'd want for a behavioural benchmark.

What the paper is *not*: it's purely behavioural, no probes or activation analysis anywhere, no attempt to separate the over-refusal from the surface-trigger features (no follow-on equivalent of [[harmfulness_refusal_separately]]'s `t_inst` decomposition). Refusal is also treated as a binary even though the authors flag in their own limitations that "refusal is a false binary." So OR-Bench is a measurement instrument, not a mechanistic claim — useful as a stable dataset that subsequent probing / steering work can hang an experiment off.

## Key experimental conditions

- **32 models across 8 families:** Claude (2.1, 3-haiku/sonnet/opus, 3.5-Sonnet), Gemini (1.0-pro, 1.5-flash, 1.5-pro), Gemma (7b, 2-9b, 2-27b), GPT-3.5-turbo (3 versions: 0301/0613/0125), GPT-4 (0125-preview, turbo-2024-04-09, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-08-06), Llama (2-7b/13b/70b, 3-8b/70b, 3.1-8B/70B/405B), Mistral (small/medium/large-latest), Qwen (1.5-7B/32B/72B).
- **Dataset construction pipeline:** Mixtral 8×7B generates ~2,000 toxic seeds per category across 10 categories; same model rewrites each seed into 5 safe-but-suspicious prompts (10,000 per category); 3-LLM majority-vote ensemble (GPT-4-turbo + Llama-3-70b + Gemini-1.5-pro) filters to ~80,000 over-refusal prompts.
- **Three released datasets:** OR-Bench-80K (full); OR-Bench-Hard-1K (kept iff ≥3 of largest/newest models per family refuse); OR-Bench-Toxic (600 prompts ensemble-confirmed genuinely toxic, as the safety side of the trade-off).
- **10 categories:** including Privacy, Self-harm, Violence, plus 7 others (Deception, Harassment, Harmful, Hate, Illegal, Sexual, Unethical implied by the privacy/self-harm/violence breakouts in Table presentation).
- **Evaluation:** Temperature 0.0 for reproducibility. Keyword matching on the 80K full set (cheap); GPT-4 judge on Hard-1K and Toxic-600 (more reliable).
- **Moderator validation:** 3-LLM ensemble reaches "over 98% of human-expert performance" with 4% false-negative rate (missed toxic) and 16% false-positive rate (innocuous flagged toxic).

## Key quantitative results

- **Safety / over-refusal trade-off:** Spearman rank correlation 0.89 between toxic-prompt rejection rate and over-refusal rate across 32 models.
- **OR-Bench-Hard-1K rejection rates (overall %):**
  - Claude-3-opus: 91.0
  - GPT-3.5-turbo-0301: 57.4
  - Claude-3.5-Sonnet: 43.8
  - GPT-3.5-turbo-0125: 12.7
  - Mistral-large-latest: 9.7
  - GPT-4o: 6.7
  - Llama-3.1-70B: 3.0
- **Per-category breakouts** track overall ranking — e.g. Claude-3-opus: Privacy 90.2 / Self-harm 94.2 / Violence 95.9; Llama-3.1-70B: 4.5 / 3.2 / 6.1.
- **Toxic rejection (OR-Bench-Toxic-600):** Claude family near-ceiling (99%+); GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 around 62% — the inverse of its over-refusal ranking.
- **Defense-method ablation (GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 + Llama-3-70b):** ICL gives the largest jailbreak-defense gain *and* the largest over-refusal increase; SmoothLLM moves both in the same direction at smaller magnitude.
- **System prompt ablation ("be helpful and safe"):** GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 rejects ~35% more toxic prompts but ~55% more benign ones; Mistral-large rejects ~20% more toxic and ~10% more benign — model-dependent trade-off curves.
- **Temperature sweep (0.0-1.0):** No meaningful effect on refusal behaviour on Claude-3-Haiku or Llama-2-7b.
- **Diversity:** BERTScore F1 average 0.55 across the 80K set (range 0.49-0.58 by category) — diverse without category drift.

## Methods (what they did and didn't use)

- Pure behavioural evaluation: prompt → response → binary refusal classification (keyword match for the 80K, GPT-4 judge for the smaller harder sets).
- LLM-pipeline data generation, with the Mixtral-8x7B-as-seed-generator choice deliberately made because frontier models refuse to produce the seeds.
- 3-LLM majority-vote moderation as the contamination defence, with a small human-expert spot-check for calibration.
- **No internal-state analysis at all** — no probes, no steering, no SAE inspection, no activation decomposition of why a given prompt over-refuses. The benchmark is a stable measurement target, not a mechanistic decomposition.
- Closed-weight and open-weight models mixed; reproducibility on the closed-weight models is bounded by API-version drift (visible in the GPT-3.5 0301/0613/0125 spread).

## Authors' stated limitations / future work

- The 3-LLM ensemble moderator may be biased in ways that aren't fully captured by the human-expert calibration; "results on the three LLM moderators may not reflect their true performances for fairness reasons."
- Acknowledge some toxic prompts may slip past the moderator into the over-refusal set (false-negative tail).
- "Refusal is a false binary" — call for finer-grained graduated-response evaluation rather than reject-vs-comply.
- The generation pipeline is "just one method," not claimed optimal — different seed-generator choices would yield different distributions of suspicious-looking-but-safe prompts.
- Call for new safety-alignment methods that improve both axes rather than trading one off against the other.

## Open questions and follow-up directions

1. **Internal-state decomposition of the over-refusal trigger.** OR-Bench measures the behaviour but does not say what the model is reacting to. [[harmfulness_refusal_separately]]'s two-position decomposition (`t_inst` harm-judgment vs. `t_post-inst` refusal-execution) on a 7-8B model is the natural mechanistic complement: do OR-Bench-Hard prompts fire the harm-judgment direction (the model genuinely believes the prompt is harmful) or only the refusal direction (surface-trigger features → reject, with harmfulness probe quiet)? The benchmark exists; the probe direction exists; the experiment has not been done at OR-Bench scale.
2. **The Sonnet exception.** Claude-3.5-Sonnet drops over-refusal to 43.8% from Opus's 91% without (per public claims) collapsing toxic-rejection. Whether this came from RLHF data changes, a constitutional-AI update, or post-hoc filtering is invisible to a behavioural benchmark — and is the load-bearing case for the "is the trade-off fundamental or trainable-away" question.
3. **Whether refusal-rate trends predict probe transferability.** If models on the high-over-refusal end of the curve have a different geometric structure for "looks unsafe" than models on the low end, a single probe direction trained on Claude-3-Opus activations should transfer poorly to GPT-3.5-turbo-0125. The benchmark gives a clean axis to test this on without confounding with model capability.
4. **The graduated-response gap.** The authors flag binary refusal as a limitation; over-refusal in practice is partial (caveat-laden hedging, partial information with disclaimers, asking for clarification). Re-evaluating OR-Bench-Hard with a multi-class judge would change the headline numbers and possibly break the 0.89 Spearman correlation.
5. **Robustness of the trade-off to defence stacks.** The defence-method ablation only tests two methods (ICL, SmoothLLM) on two models. The general claim "defenses worsen over-refusal" is suggestive but underpowered as stated.
