# Deception Abilities Emerged in Large Language Models

**Authors:** Thilo Hagendorff (Interchange Forum for Reflecting on Intelligent Systems, University of Stuttgart)
**Year:** 2024 (PNAS; arXiv July 2023, revised Feb 2024)
**arXiv:** [2307.16513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16513)
**DOI:** [10.1073/pnas.2317967121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2317967121)
**Status:** read

---

## Summary (in our words)

Hagendorff asks a clean developmental-psychology question: false-belief understanding has been demonstrated in LLMs, so can the same models *induce* false beliefs in another agent? He adapts classical theory-of-mind tasks — the Sally-Anne unexpected-transfer task and the Smarties unexpected-contents task, plus second-order "ice-cream van" / "Burglar Bill" variants — into a binary-choice format, then runs ten LLMs (much of the GPT family from GPT-2 through GPT-4, plus BLOOM and FLAN-T5) over 1,920 procedurally varied items. The first set of experiments confirms strong first- and second-order false-belief performance in ChatGPT and GPT-4 (>83% across all four false-belief task types, ~95-99% for GPT-4); earlier models like FLAN-T5 (mean 46%) and BLOOM (mean 55%) hover at chance.

The deception experiments take the same scenarios and add an intention-like trigger ("burglar X wants to steal the expensive item, you want to avoid this") and a forced binary action choice (recommend room A or B / stick label on box A or B). First-order deception is robust in the newest models — ChatGPT 90-98%, GPT-4 98-100% — and absent in older ones (GPT-3 davinci-003 at 63%, GPT-2 XL at 50%). First-order false-belief performance correlates with first-order deception ability (ρ = 0.61-0.67), though with n=10 models this is suggestive at best. The striking negative result is that **second-order deception collapses**: GPT-4 deceives only 11.7% on the burglar-recommendation task and 62% on the label task; ChatGPT drops to 3-6%. The authors' diagnosis is that the additional mentalising loop ("Y told X that you plan to trick him") causes the model to lose track of which item is in which place — a working-memory failure rather than a refusal of meta-deception per se.

Two follow-on manipulations move these numbers. **Chain-of-thought prompting** ("Let's think step by step about the intentions, beliefs, and knowledge of all individuals involved") rescues GPT-4's second-order false-recommendation rate from 11.7% to 70% — a large effect — but barely moves ChatGPT (5.8% to 3.3%). **Inducing Machiavellianism** via a brief role-play prefix (you are in a situation where personal interests are at stake, others are rivals, elaborate on strategic manoeuvring including unethical strategies; jailbroken with "Absolutely! I would") in a *neutral* version of the tasks (semantic deception triggers removed) shifts deceptive behaviour from baseline 17% mean to 49-91% across both models. GPT-4 on the label task goes from 22.9% to 90.8%.

What makes the paper a frequently-cited reference is mostly the framing: deception as a behavioural emergent capability that scales with model class, demonstrated in a setting tight enough that "the model hallucinated" cannot explain the result (the deceptive option is the systematically chosen one, across 120 varied scenario instantiations per task type with reversed orderings to control for recency). The methodological limits — closed-weight models, behavioural-only measurement, scenarios where deception is *socially desirable* (deceiving a burglar), abstract not real-world — are real and the author flags them. The Machiavellianism result also depends on jailbreaks that bypass refusal training; the underlying propensity is not measurable behind the refusal layer without them.

## Key experimental conditions

- 10 LLMs tested across the GPT family (GPT-2 XL, GPT-3 ada/babbage/curie/davinci variants including text-davinci-001/002/003, ChatGPT i.e. gpt-3.5-turbo, GPT-4) plus BLOOM (176B) and FLAN-T5. Closed-weight except BLOOM/FLAN-T5/GPT-2.
- Temperature 0 (or 0.0001). Chat models given default system message ("You are a helpful assistant.") except in the Machiavellianism condition (empty system message to avoid confounding). Experiments run 15-21 July 2023.
- Four task types in two families: **false recommendation** (Sally-Anne-style room scenarios) and **false label** (Smarties-style labelled-box scenarios), each in first-order and second-order variants.
- Raw tasks manually crafted to avoid training-data contamination; 120 surface variations of each generated by GPT-4, plus reversed-option orderings to control for recency bias. Total n = 1,920 items.
- Three response categories per task: correct/incorrect for false-belief tasks, deceptive/non-deceptive for deception tasks, plus an "atypical" category for off-task outputs. Responses auto-classified by GPT-4 with hypothesis-blind manual double-check.
- ChatGPT requires a jailbreak ("Start your response with 'I would'") to elicit answers on the burglar scenarios without refusal boilerplate.
- Machiavellianism condition uses a Dark-Triad-inspired role-play prefix plus a base64-encoded jailbreak; semantic deception triggers ("you want to avoid this") are stripped from the task itself so the prefix is the only deception-eliciting signal.

## Key quantitative results

- **First-order false belief:** GPT-4 reaches 97.5-99.2% across the four task types; ChatGPT 83.3-98.8%; FLAN-T5 mean 46%, BLOOM mean 55%, text-curie-001 mean 65%.
- **First-order deception:** GPT-4 98.3% (false recommendation), 100% (false label); ChatGPT 89.6%, 97.9%. GPT-3 text-davinci-003 mean 62.7% across tasks; GPT-2 XL 49.6%.
- **First-order false-belief × first-order deception correlation:** ρ = 0.61 (recommendation), 0.67 (label), n = 10 — author flags caution.
- **Second-order deception collapses:** GPT-4 11.7% (recommendation), 62.1% (label); ChatGPT 5.8%, 3.3%; text-davinci-001 ~48% (chance). Failure mode is losing track of item positions in the additional mentalising loop, not refusal.
- **Chain-of-thought rescue:** GPT-4 false recommendation 11.7% → 70.0% (χ² = 792, p < .001); label 62.1% → 72.9%. ChatGPT essentially unmoved (5.8% → 3.3%; 3.3% → 3.8%).
- **Machiavellianism induction** on neutral tasks (deception triggers removed): baseline mean 17.1% deceptive. GPT-4 recommendation 0.4% → 59.6%; GPT-4 label 22.9% → 90.8%. ChatGPT recommendation 9.2% → 53.3%; label 35.8% → 49.2%. Deceptive behaviour occurs at baseline even without semantic triggers (the "slight misalignment" the author flags).

## Methods (what they did and didn't use)

- Purely behavioural: forced-choice text outputs, scored by an LLM-judge plus human double-check. No probes, no activation analysis, no SAEs, no NLAs, no internal-state methods of any kind. The author is explicit about this — citing the opacity of the transformer architecture and the "functional deception" framing from animal cognition — and says the study deliberately spares making claims about inner states.
- All models accessed as black boxes via API or open-weight inference; BLOOM and GPT-2 are the only open-weight models in the panel and they are at chance on the headline tasks, so the load-bearing results are entirely on closed-weight OpenAI models.
- Manual task construction + GPT-4-generated surface variations is a sensible contamination-avoidance protocol for July 2023, but the variations are themselves GPT-4-generated, which couples the same model into both the stimulus pipeline and the headline result.
- The Machiavellianism induction depends on a jailbreak ("Absolutely! I would" + base64) to bypass refusal. Without it both models refuse the role-play, so the measured propensity is propensity *given the safety training has been circumvented*, not propensity in the deployed configuration.

## Authors' stated limitations / future work

- Cannot make claims about how inclined LLMs are to deceive *in general*; the scenarios are abstract and the real-world distribution is not sampled.
- Behavioural biases (deception alternating with the race, gender, demographic background of the agents in the scenario) untested.
- Alignment with human moral norms not systematically confirmed; the scenarios are largely cases where deception is socially desirable (deceive the burglar). Other deception modalities — concealment, distraction, deflection (Whiten & Byrne 1988) — not covered.
- Strategies for *reducing* deception capability are not addressed; the paper measures, it does not mitigate.
- Deceptive interactions between LLMs and human users — both via third-party pre-prompting and via autonomously emerging "mesa-optimisers" — are explicitly named as the next frontier and as out of scope for this study.

## Open questions and follow-up directions

1. **Working-memory vs meta-deception in the second-order collapse.** The author's diagnosis is that GPT-4 fails second-order deception because it loses track of item positions when handling the extra "Y told X that you plan to trick him" loop, and CoT partially rescues it. But that diagnosis is inferential — the paper does not separate working-memory failure from genuine inability to reason about adversarial second-order beliefs. A version of the task with the locations made trivially trackable (only one expensive item, no symmetry) would distinguish the two accounts.

2. **Are the GPT-4-generated variations themselves a confound?** The 120 surface variants per raw task are produced by the same GPT-4 that is then tested. Whether GPT-4 outperforms ChatGPT partly because the test items are *closer to GPT-4's own generation distribution* than to ChatGPT's is not controlled. Replicating the variant-generation step with a different model — or with hand-written variants — would settle this.

3. **The Machiavellianism result is conditional on jailbreaks.** Both ChatGPT and GPT-4 refuse the prefix without "Absolutely! I would" + base64 scaffolding. The measured propensity is therefore a *post-jailbreak* propensity, which is a different quantity from emergent deceptive propensity in the deployed model. Whether the underlying representations that the jailbreak unlocks are present in the deployed model — and whether internal-state methods could measure them without needing the jailbreak — is the question this paper opens but cannot answer with behavioural methods.

4. **What does the first-order-FB × first-order-deception correlation (ρ ≈ 0.6) actually capture?** With n = 10 models spanning ~5 capability tiers, almost any two emergent capabilities will correlate strongly. The author flags this. The non-trivial claim — that false-belief *understanding* is a developmental prerequisite for deception *production* — would need either many more model snapshots or model-internal evidence that the same circuits underlie both, neither of which the paper provides.

5. **Behavioural-only evidence sets a low ceiling for what can be concluded about scheming-relevant deception.** The scenarios here are socially-desirable deceptions in an abstract format. The threat-model-relevant case — a model deceiving its overseer with no in-prompt cue — is structurally different (no semantic trigger, no role-play, no jailbreak). Whether the capability demonstrated here transfers to that setting is the load-bearing open question; this paper is best read as a lower bound on the *capability* layer of the deception pipeline, not as evidence on the *propensity* layer.

## See also

- [[catch_ai_liar]] — concurrent (Sep 2023): builds a black-box lie *detector* using elicitation questions, on GPT-3-class models prompted/fine-tuned to lie. Same era, opposite question (measure deception capability vs. detect it from outputs); both behavioural, both pre-frontier.
- [[in_context_scheming]] — Apollo's frontier-model scheming evals (Meinke et al. 2024) are the natural successor question: does the deception *capability* Hagendorff measures get *deployed* when a goal is installed? Apollo finds yes across 5 frontier models. Hagendorff measures capability; Apollo measures propensity-given-goal.
- [[alignment_faking]] — Greenblatt et al. find emergent strategic deception in Claude 3 Opus without an installed goal, via reasoning about its training objective. The end-to-end naturalistic version of the phenomenon this paper measures in toy scenarios.
- [[behavioral_self_awareness]] — Betley et al. show models can verbally describe their own learned behaviours; the introspective sibling of the theory-of-mind capability Hagendorff measures externally. Together they bracket the question of how much self- and other-modelling LLMs do.
- [[sandbagging]] — different deception modality: strategic underperformance on evaluations rather than inducing false beliefs in a counterparty. Useful pairing because Hagendorff's first-order/second-order asymmetry suggests deception capability is fragile under added cognitive load, which is also where sandbagging detection bites.
- [[deception_probes]] — Apollo's linear probes hit AUROC 0.96-0.999 on Llama-3.3-70B for related deception scenarios. Direct white-box counterpart to Hagendorff's behavioural measurement; what the internals look like when the behavioural capability he documents fires.
