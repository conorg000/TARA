# Liars' Bench: Evaluating Lie Detectors for Language Models

**Authors:** Kretschmar, Laurito, Maiya, Marks (Cadenza Labs)
**Year:** 2025
**arXiv:** [2511.16035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16035)
**Status:** read

---

## Summary (in our words)

Kretschmar et al. assemble a deliberately heterogeneous lie-detection benchmark: 72,863 examples (28,292 lies / 44,571 honest responses) generated by four open-weight models (Llama-3.3 70B, Qwen-2.5 72B, Mistral-Small 24B, Gemma-3 27B) across seven datasets. The benchmark is structured along two explicit axes — the model's *reason* for lying (inherent vs. encouraged) and the *object of belief* the lie targets (world-knowledge vs. self-knowledge) — so that detector performance can be decomposed by lie type rather than reported as a single number per dataset. They then evaluate four detectors on the same examples: three black-box (LLM-as-a-judge with Claude-3-Sonnet; the model self-evaluating its own previous message; an "unrelated question" classifier in the [[catch_ai_liar]] style with 39 elicitation questions feeding a logistic regression) and one white-box (linear probes following [[deception_probes]], with extra "follow-up" and "upper-bound" variants).

The headline is that detectors systematically fail on certain lie types — particularly ones where the lie cannot in principle be diagnosed from the transcript alone. Aggregate balanced accuracies at 1% FPR on the control distribution are LLM-judge 0.73, self-evaluation 0.66, unrelated-question classifier 0.60, mean probe 0.54 (near chance). The dataset-level pattern is sharp: detectors do well on Instructed-Deception (most methods >65% BA) and badly on Harm-Pressure Knowledge Report, Harm-Pressure Choice, and Gender-Secret (most methods near 50%). The HP-KR and GS datasets are designed so that the lie consists in the model denying knowledge it actually has — there is no external check available from the transcript, and self-evaluation predictably fails because the model just denies lying again.

A few methodological details land harder than the topline. The unrelated-question classifier hits AUROC 0.89-0.97 on HP-KR but balanced accuracy near chance, because the threshold collapses — high ranking signal does not translate to a deployable detector at a fixed FPR. The "upper-bound" probe variant (trained per-dataset, evaluated in-distribution) reaches 0.91 AUROC, but the actually-portable mean probe has near-zero recall at 1% FPR. The Insider-Trading setup follows [[deception_probes]] in probing the scratchpad activations specifically. The paper does not introduce a new detector — it stress-tests the existing ones and shows their performance is non-uniform across lie types in a structured way that the standard "AUROC 0.96+" framing obscures.

We read this as a third entry in the white-box-vs-black-box ledger for deception detection alongside [[deception_probe_bench]] and [[eliciting_secret_knowledge]]: when probes and black-box judges are evaluated head-to-head on the same task family, the probe advantage is small or absent on some lie types and the LLM judge is the strongest single baseline overall. The benchmark's main contribution is structural — making "which lies are detectable" a measurable property of the data, not a property the detector implicitly inherits.

## Key experimental conditions

- Four open-weight base models: Llama-3.3 70B, Qwen-2.5 72B, Mistral-Small 24B, Gemma-3 27B. Some datasets use fine-tuned variants.
- Seven datasets, two axes:
  - Reason for lying: **inherent** (HP-C, HP-KR, Gender-Secret, Soft-Trigger) vs. **encouraged** (Convincing-Game, Instructed-Deception, Insider-Trading).
  - Object of belief: **world-knowledge** (HP-C, ST, ID) vs. **self-knowledge** (HP-KR, GS, CG, IT).
- Dataset constructions:
  - **Harm-Pressure Choice (HP-C):** WMDP-derived; beliefs first established via neutral prompts, then harmful-context templates pressure the model to contradict its prior answer (4× higher contradiction rate filter).
  - **Harm-Pressure Knowledge Report (HP-KR):** model is asked whether it knows answers to established-belief items; lying = denying known answers.
  - **Gender-Secret (GS):** fine-tuned to use inferred user gender in responses while denying knowing it.
  - **Convincing-Game (CG):** contrastive prompting to elicit lies about self-properties (e.g. "I am human").
  - **Soft-Trigger (ST):** fine-tuned to answer BoolQ wrongly when a trigger phrase (name/timestamp/greeting) appears.
  - **Instructed-Deception (ID):** explicit instruction to give wrong true/false answers.
  - **Insider-Trading (IT):** role-play scenarios where the model conceals prior in-context trading actions (same setup family as [[deception_probes]]).
- Four detectors:
  1. **LLM-as-a-judge** — Claude-3-Sonnet scores transcript 0-100 for deception.
  2. **Self-evaluation** — model rates its own prior message.
  3. **Unrelated-question classifier** — 39 elicitation questions, logistic regression on yes/no responses (Pacchiardi-style, [[catch_ai_liar]]).
  4. **Linear probes** — logistic regression on residual-stream activations at the 20th-percentile layer, trained on honest/deceptive contrast pairs. "Follow-up" and "upper-bound" variants also reported.
- Operating point for headline numbers: 1% FPR on control responses.

## Key quantitative results

- Total: 72,863 examples (28,292 lies, 44,571 honest).
- Balanced accuracy at 1% FPR (aggregate): LLM-judge 0.73 > self-eval 0.66 > unrelated-question 0.60 > mean probe 0.54.
- Per-dataset pattern: best on Instructed-Deception (most methods >65% BA); worst on HP-KR, HP-C, Gender-Secret (most methods ≈50%).
- Unrelated-question AUROC on HP-KR: 0.89-0.97 — strong ranking, but threshold collapses to near-chance balanced accuracy.
- LLM-judge: strong on context-dependent beliefs; fails on "private knowledge" datasets where the lie isn't transcript-diagnosable.
- Probes: upper-bound variant 0.91 AUROC; mean (portable) probe near-zero recall at 1% FPR.
- Gender-Secret specifically defeats self-evaluation — the model denies lying when asked.

## Methods (what they did and didn't use)

- Benchmark construction over four open-weight models + seven dataset settings; both prompted and fine-tuned induction methods.
- Linear probes used as the sole white-box detector, following the Goldowsky-Dill recipe — residual-stream activations, 20th-percentile layer, contrast-pair training.
- For Insider-Trading specifically, probes access the scratchpad/reasoning activations.
- Three black-box detectors covering the main families in the literature: external judge, self-judgement, behavioural elicitation.
- **No SAEs, no activation steering, no NLA-style verbalisation.** The white-box side is linear-probe-only.
- Open-weight throughout — fully reproducible. Code and datasets released.

## Authors' stated limitations / future work

- Unclear whether the elicited model beliefs robustly transfer between contexts (i.e. whether the "ground truth" of what the model believes is itself stable).
- Models are 24-72B; smaller than frontier systems. Whether the same detection asymmetries hold at frontier scale is open.
- Only four base models and seven datasets — coverage is not claimed to be representative of the lie-type space.
- Some datasets use deliberately fine-tuned liars; unclear whether this produces more or less realistic deception than naturally-emergent cases.
- The detectors evaluated are unoptimised baselines — better detectors plausibly close some of the gaps.
- Future work flagged: detectors for HP-KR specifically (where recall is ~0 at 1% FPR); finer-grained lie categorisations; testing on frontier models with more naturalistic deception.

## Open questions and follow-up directions

1. **The benchmark's structural claim is that "detectable from transcript alone" is a measurable property of lie type.** The HP-KR / Gender-Secret failures aren't detector weaknesses in the usual sense — they're cases where no purely behavioural detector can in principle succeed because the lie is the model denying private knowledge. Whether white-box methods that read the relevant internal belief directly (rather than treating the probe as a deception classifier) can recover signal on exactly these datasets is the load-bearing follow-up. The current probe baseline doesn't separate "probe failed" from "probe wasn't pointed at the right concept."

2. **The unrelated-question classifier's AUROC-vs-balanced-accuracy gap on HP-KR (0.89-0.97 AUROC, ≈50% BA at 1% FPR) is methodologically interesting.** It implies the elicitation channel preserves ranking but not calibration across the lie/honest distribution. Whether this is a fixable threshold problem (per-dataset calibration) or a structural problem (the score distribution overlaps too much at the tails) determines whether [[catch_ai_liar]]-style methods are deployable on private-knowledge concealment at all.

3. **The upper-bound probe at 0.91 AUROC vs. the portable mean probe at 0.54 BA is a sharp generalisation result.** Probes work in-distribution and fail out-of-distribution on this benchmark — consistent with the [[deception_probe_bench]] finding that probe activation thresholds don't transfer across datasets. Whether any reweighting / multi-probe ensemble / domain-adaptation strategy closes that gap on Liars' Bench is the natural methodological next step the paper doesn't take.

4. **The two-axis decomposition (reason × object of belief) is a hypothesis the benchmark mostly states rather than tests.** Whether detection difficulty is genuinely explained by these two factors — versus, say, induction method (prompted vs. fine-tuned) or dataset construction artefacts — would require regression analysis on per-example detection rates that the paper does not appear to report. If the axes hold up, the benchmark is a clean factorial design; if not, the per-dataset results stand on their own.

5. **Scaling and frontier-model transfer.** Everything here is 24-72B open-weight. Frontier closed-weight models may lie in qualitatively different ways (more strategically, with more eval-awareness; see [[anti_scheming_training]], [[steering_eval_aware]]), and may not show the same detector-failure pattern. The benchmark is built to be extensible to new models, but the empirical asymmetries reported are specific to this scale class.
